A second vault — [docket-testbed](https://github.com/vadymdidenkolab/docket-testbed) — modelled
on the shape of a real board rather than on our defaults, so that docket is exercised against
a team it did not design itself.

[[DKT-1 Vault format and the starter vault]] shows what a vault looks like. This shows what one looks like
after a team has had it for a quarter: an eight-stage deployment pipeline with a workflow that
moves forward one stage and back to «В работе» when a stage fails; types, statuses and
priorities in the team's own language; four epics with sub-tasks under them; all seven typed
relations; every label a page; and six weeks of history by four authors with three tags and two
proposals on branches. Everything in it is invented.

It found two things in a day, both of which look almost right on a three-column English vault:
the board's columns came out alphabetically, and a new task got the first priority in the list,
which in a list written low-to-high is the lowest. See [[DKT-33 Columns in the order the workflow says]].

The point is that neither was findable by reading the code. A vault with our own vocabulary
agrees with us about everything.

## Acceptance

- [x] A pipeline of eight statuses with a workflow that is not anything-to-anything.
- [x] A vocabulary — types, statuses, priorities — in the team's own language.
- [x] Hierarchy, all seven relations, labels as pages, non-ASCII paths and refs.
- [x] A history worth reading: tags, branches, four authors.
