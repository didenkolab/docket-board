Dragging worked in the sense that a card moved, and badly in every sense that matters. And the
task page was a property sheet with a body underneath: correct, and nothing like the page
anyone opens forty times a day.

## Acceptance

- [x] A card can be dragged into any column the workflow allows, with somewhere to aim.
- [x] The drag shows where the card will land before it lands.
- [x] The task page reads as an issue: breadcrumb, title, status, description, activity, details.
- [x] Comments are shown as comments, not as more description.

## Comments

**agent/claude · 2026-08-30 18:16** — The drag was rebuilt after reading how the earlier
prototype did it, which was the right instinct: the fault was structural, not a missing tweak.

A column was a left rule with no body, so an empty one was a line of text and there was nowhere
to aim. It is now a box with a header and a body that keeps a floor height. The card was an
anchor, so the browser dragged a link with a ghost URL; naming the card as the drag image fixes
that without giving up the plain link that makes the board work with JavaScript off. A dashed
placeholder opens where the card will land — a drag without one is a guess you resolve by
letting go.

The task page follows the arrangement people already know: breadcrumb, title, a status control
directly under it, description and activity on the left, details on the right. Familiar beats
clever on a page opened that often.

That meant reading comments back out of the file, since they are stored as text under a
heading rather than as structured data. It is a parse rather than a lookup, which is the trade
the format makes: the conversation stays readable to anyone who opens the file, and the cost
lands here.

One real defect found by looking rather than by testing: `.block h2` was styling the headings
inside a description as section labels, so an author's `## Acceptance` came out as interface
chrome. Scoped to direct children.
